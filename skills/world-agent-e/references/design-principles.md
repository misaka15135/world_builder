# Architectural decisions for explicit cuboid designs

Start from the type's actual function and site frontage. Lay out public entry,
private/service areas and all required vertical circulation before facades.
Large halls may be one space; dwellings may need several. Room count is not an
aesthetic score. Give every intended occupied level a usable path.

Choose a coherent silhouette: a dominant hall with secondary service volume,
courtyard, stepped roof or tower can be expressed by multiple non-overlapping
cuboids. A pitched roof can use explicit stepped courses. Decide the massing
from function and the user's cultural/era context, not a universal formula.

Make the entrance recognisable from the road. Give windows actual clear gaps or
glass components, distribute them according to room use, and retain enough
solid wall for readable rhythm. Model depth through bases, eaves, piers and
recesses within the envelope. Do not fill a window opening with oversized trim.

Use a legible material hierarchy and daylight openings. The palette's glass is
solid for traversal; white is white concrete, not an emissive light. There is no
lighting simulation or structural engineering validation. Avoid unsupported
claims about historical precedent; sources supplied in the prompt are context,
not independently verified citations.

Review both plan and street silhouette before responding. Source-route acceptance
does not prove engine-specific collision behaviour, beauty, realistic structure,
fire safety or construction suitability.
